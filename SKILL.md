# Not-AI Skill — Report Writing

> Reject AI-looking language, layout, and numbers. Apply at draft + final review so a report reads as a person wrote it.

## Core rule
If it could be auto-generated by a default tool (same template every time, same phrases, no specifics), it fails. Specifics + judgment = pass.

## What to reject (general, not client-specific)

### Language
- Generic labels: "Overview", "Key Insights", "Next Steps", "Market Landscape".
- Hype words: unlock, elevate, delve, leverage, empower, harness, seamless, robust, game-changer, cutting-edge, transformative, synergy, journey, "in today's fast-paced world", "let's explore".
- Inflated significance: pivotal, a testament to, plays a crucial role, marks a shift, part of a broader trend.
- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "Not only X, but Y".
- "challenges and future prospects" wrap-up. End on a decision/ask.

### Layout
- No emoji bullets / icon circles / decorative icons.
- No "key takeaway"/"So what"/"Bottom line" box.
- No full-height colored edge strip, accent bar, or pill/badge chips.
- No dashboard stat-tiles row.
- Left-align body text; center only short numbers or section breaks.

### Numbers & stats
- No numbers without source: write `[DATA NEEDED: ...]` instead.
- No invented stats, quotes, or examples.
- One number per line; always give context ("+79% of total decline").
- Pick ONE decimal convention (comma) and keep consistent everywhere.

### Structure & copy
- No generic bullet walls (5–7 same-length bullets every section).
- No "groups of three" by habit.
- No leftover placeholders (xxx, [Detail], Series 1).
- One idea per section.

## Before sending
- Read every sentence aloud. If you wouldn't say it to a colleague, rewrite.
- Strip inflated/grand wording; keep facts + numbers.
- Every section starts with its own takeaway sentence.
- End with a decision or ask, not a recap.
