# Guidelines: Rule of Thumbs for Consultant-Standard Reporting Decks 

## 1. Strategic Framing and Thematic Grounding 

In management consulting, the initial framing of a report is an exercise in intellectual sovereignty. It is the "anchor" that dictates whether a document is perceived as a mere data summary or a definitive strategic mandate. Top-tier firms like McKinsey and EY avoid descriptive titles in favor of evocative, forward-leaning narratives. By branding a report as "The Next Productivity Frontier" or "Work Reimagined," consultants transition the reader from a passive observation of industry trends to an active engagement with a shifting paradigm. This thematic grounding ensures that complex data sets are viewed as evidence of an inescapable future rather than isolated statistics. **Rule of Thumb: Commands for Strategic Grounding** 

   - **Suppress Descriptive Headers; Elevate Strategic Declarations:** Move away from functional titles like "AI and Norwegian Jobs." Instead, utilize titles that signal evolution and high-value states, such as "The Economic Potential of Generative AI." 

   - **Anchor Broad Trends in Quantifiable Impacts:** Immediately bridge global technological shifts to specific economic or organizational outcomes. Transition from "GenAI in Norway" (the trend) to "Value Creation Potential of 95-159 bn NOK" (the localized impact). 

   - **Establish the Narrative Perimeter:** Use proprietary markers (e.g., "Confidential and Proprietary") and precise timestamps (e.g., "June 2023") to signal that the document offers privileged, time-sensitive insights tailored for a high-value audience. **The "So What?" of Credibility Markers** The use of "Confidential" watermarks and specific dates is not administrative filler; it is a credibility signal. These markers suggest that the insights are fresh, exclusive, and high-stakes. This framing provides the necessary psychological foundation for the Executive Summary, which must immediately capitalize on this perceived authority to drive decision-making. 

2. High-Impact Executive Summarization 

The Executive Summary is a strategic necessity, functioning as a stand-alone resource for C-suite readers who require high-level momentum synthesized with granular value potential. It must serve as a microcosm of the entire engagement, lead with the most disruptive findings, and provide a clear roadmap for action. Its purpose is to compress the "What" and the "Why" into a narrative that compels the reader to investigate the "How." **Guide to** 

**Consultant-Standard Summary Bullets** 

- **The Monetary Lead (Range as Risk Management):** Lead with specific value ranges to ground the impact in currency while acknowledging variance. 

- _Example:_ "GenAI is expected to unlock a value creation potential of 95-159 bn NOK by 2045, with roughly 60% of that value realized by 2030." 

- **The Sentiment/Risk Lead:** Use employee sentiment to highlight immediate organizational threats. 

- _Example:_ "A majority (54%) of employees are likely to quit if flexibility preferences are not met; crucially, Millennials are two times as likely as Baby Boomers to resign, creating a significant leadership pipeline risk." 

- **The Specificity Pivot:** Transition from total industry volume to the most disruptive functional shifts. 

- _Example:_ "While Energy holds the highest absolute potential (~21 bn NOK), High Tech will undergo the most disruptive transformation (7.0% impact on industry revenue)." **The "So What?" of Key Findings** Consultant reporting must move beyond reporting "data" to extracting "insight." Noting that Norway is the 10th highest educated country is a fact; concluding that this high baseline "increases the feasibility of early adoption" is a strategic judgment. Once the summary establishes these high stakes, the deck must then define the conceptual framework and technical perimeter of the subject. 

## 3. Defining the "What" and Logical Segmentation 

Baseline definitions are the foundation of a shared language between the consultant and the client. A professional definition must define the _perimeter_ —explicitly stating what the technology or index covers and where it fails. McKinsey establishes this by contrasting Generative AI's creative capabilities against "unsuitable" applications, while EY uses the Remote Work Preference Index (RPI) to quantify abstract desires into a clear 1–3 scale (ranging from 1 for "Old Normal" to 3 for "New Normal"). **Instructions for Building Suitability Tables and Archetypes** When categorizing complex features, use an archetype model that includes a mandatory "Constraints/Risks" column to mirror the technology's perimeter: 

   - **Content Synthesis (Virtual Expert):** Summarizing unstructured data to generate insights. _Constraint:_ Unsuited for high-stakes scenarios with potential for harm. 

   - **Coding & Software:** Generating and refactoring code to reduce technical debt. _Constraint:_ Currently unsuitable for complex numerical reasoning without oversight. 

   - **Creative Content:** Ideation and personalized marketing copy generation. _Constraint:_ Risk of exposing biased or harmful content in unconstrained, long-form generation. 

   - **Customer Engagement:** Streamlining interactions via chatbots. _Constraint:_ Unsuitable for applications requiring absolute explainability and full understanding of failure modes. **The "So What?" of Segmentation Strategy** Segmenting impact by both "Industry" and "Business Function" makes the data actionable. Identifying value in "Marketing and Sales" (28-43 bn NOK) or "Software Engineering" (21-43 bn NOK) is more valuable than a broad "Retail" sector claim. It allows leadership to allocate resources to specific departments where the "generation" of content, code, or emails will drive the highest ROI. Defining the "What" through these functional lenses permits a deeper dive into "The Who" via demographic and cohort analysis. 

4. Human-Centric Nuance and Cohort Analysis 

High-value reports must account for the human element to remain relevant to executive leadership. Specificity in human data—referencing EY's 24 demographic categories or McKinsey’s focus on the Norwegian "Knowledge-worker"—transforms academic theory into organizational strategy. By moving from "employees" to specific "cohorts," a report predicts behavior with far greater accuracy. **Rule of Thumb: Demographic-Driven Strategy** 

- **Quantify DESI Index Advantages:** Link a nation’s digital foundation (e.g., Norway ranking 5th in the DESI Index) to the economic feasibility of rapid adoption. 

- **Segment into Actionable Personas:** Group data to illustrate how different segments react to change. Use the EY cohorts as a model: 

- **Office Optimal:** 13% likelihood to quit; expects 0-1 days remote work. 

- **Hybrid Hopeful:** 33% likelihood to quit; expects 2-3 days remote work. 

- **Remote Ready:** 67% likelihood to quit; expects 4+ days remote work. **The "So What?" of "Day-in-the-Life" Metrics** Critiquing the "Typical Working Day" is a powerful tool for justifying productivity claims. For instance, a junior lawyer spends **45%** of their day writing documents—an activity previously considered to have lower automation potential because it required "Applying Expertise." By showing that GenAI now targets nearly half of the billable day in a high-wage profession, you transform an abstract tech trend into a concrete "Productivity Uplift" of 34 percentage points for expert tasks. 

## 5. The Consultant’s Reporting Cheatsheet (Synthesis) 

A consultant-standard deck is a tool for decision-making, not a data repository. Every visual and data point must move the reader closer to a choice. The final synthesis combines the strategic framing, executive insights, and human nuances into a cohesive, actionable plan. **Rule of Thumbs Cheatsheet** | Feature | Pattern | Consultant Rule of Thumb || ------ | ------ | ------ || **Scenario Planning** | Early vs. Late Adoption | **Anchor** in the aggressive scenario (50% automation by 2030) to highlight potential, then ground with the late scenario (2045+) for risk management. || **Data Visualization** | Point-to-Point CAGR | **Quantify** the advancement. Show how GenAI elevates productivity growth from a 1.7% "without GenAI" baseline to 3.8% in an early adoption scenario. || **Urgency Creation** | Before vs. After Shifts | **Segment** the shift in technical potential. Highlight that automation potential for Bachelor's holders jumps from 36% to 60% with GenAI. || **Sector Prioritization** | Median Impact Bubbles | **Scale** visuals proportionally. Use bubble sizes based on billion NOK impact (e.g., Energy vs. Healthcare) to allow for immediate visual prioritization. | 

**The "So What?" of Comparative Data: The Disruptive Anchor** Comparative data creates the necessary sense of urgency for strategic response. Highlighting that the number of Norwegians in roles with >50% automation potential has jumped from **2 in 7** to **1 in 2** serves as a "Disruptive Anchor." This single data point summarizes the shift from incremental change to total workplace reinvention.Professional reporting transforms raw context into a stand-alone strategic resource by ensuring every data point answers the question: "What should we do next?"
